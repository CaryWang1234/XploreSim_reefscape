G421 (1 defender at a time) is charged, and it is the reason the Defend seats are opt-in: over npm run verify's 18000 replay frames of 24 MATCHes, 883 held a single ROBOT on the opponent's half outside the BARGE ZONES and none held a second, so two greedy fleets give the clause nothing to do. §7.4.4's three clauses are mutually exclusive by the manual's own note, so one collision is one call and only the most punitive one: G422 needs a COMPONENT outside the ROBOT PERIMETER, which no machine in this build has (the box R104 allows and nothing on it), so it is blocked by construction; G423 is damage or functional impairment, whether deliberate (item A) or simply by initiating contact (item B); and G424 needs the tip to be deliberate, because "tipping as an unintended consequence of normal ROBOT to ROBOT interaction" is explicitly not a violation. Both G423 and G424 are MAJOR FOUL + YELLOW CARD, or + RED CARD if the opponent cannot drive — and §6.6.2 makes a second YELLOW in the same MATCH a RED, which DISABLES that ROBOT for the remainder. Guard 23 drives a pressing fleet with the damage floor dropped to 20 in/s and the roll forced, and requires all of it: one call per collision, a card on every one, an impairment on the victim, a DISQUALIFICATION, and none of it with contactFouls off.

What is a rule

Field dimensions, element sizes and heights, point values, ranking-point definitions, foul severities, the 15 s AUTO / 135 s TELEOP clock, the 126 CORAL and 18 ALGAE staging, G409's one-CORAL-one-ALGAE carry limit, R103's 115 lb and R104's 120 in perimeter / 42 in height. Each is quoted with its clause in the tables above. The HUMAN PLAYER's part is rules too: 1 NET and 1 PROCESSOR per ALLIANCE (§5.4.2, §5.5), an ALGAE through a PROCESSOR rolls into the opponent's PROCESSOR AREA (§5.5), a PROCESSOR AREA holds at most 4 ALGAE (G435), no ALGAE may be entered during AUTO (G404), and only a HUMAN PLAYER may put them in (G433B). Scoring them is a different list: §6.1 gives both the PROCESSOR and the NET to the ROBOT, and gives the NET to the HUMAN PLAYER as well once the opponent has passed a ring through their PROCESSOR. The pieces' shapes are rules as well: §5.7.1's CORAL is an 11 in length of 4 in diameter white PVC and §5.7.2's ALGAE is a 16-16.5 in inflated ring, so the drawing gives an ALGAE four times the CORAL's diameter, and the NET hangs from the top of the BARGE on its own ALLIANCE's face (§5.4.2).

What is an estimate

  • ROBOT tempo. Drive speed, pick time, per-level placement time, the per-level odds a CORAL does not stay on (placeDrop) and the aim error at which those odds start rising (placeTolDeg), strip time, climb time, turn rate and reliability are model assumptions in web/js/model.js, not rules. So is the drivetrain: swerve / tank / mecanum set the sideways and reverse fractions and how the spin ramps up, and R103/R104 say nothing about either. And so is the per-MATCH machine spread (Machine spread, ±20% by default) that lets two copies of one design be different robots. They are what a real team would measure in the pit.
  • Contact severity floors. §7.4.4 says "damaging or functionally impairing" and "tipping", not how hard: the in/s that counts as impairment, the fraction of speed a victim loses and for how long, and the threshold above which a bump becomes a card are this build's, placed against npm run contact's measured distribution of its own collisions rather than against a clause.
  • Field furniture placement. The manual gives element sizes but the exact station / processor / CORAL-MARK offsets live in the field drawings, which are not transcribed yet. The NET is drawn on its own half of the BARGE band because §5.4.2 hangs it from the top of the truss; where along the 12 ft is not stated.
  • L1 trough capacity as a number (24). The manual scores the trough by contact and support, not by slot count.
  • HUMAN PLAYER rates: one NET throw per 2.2 s at 75%, one CORAL through the CHUTE per 1.4 s. The 4-ALGAE area limit is a rule; how fast they get thrown is not.

The ALGAE chain

Reproduce every number in this card with npm run measure (n=24 mirror MATCHes, seedBase 9000). Processing an ALGAE is worth 6 to you and 4 x (the opponent's throw accuracy) to them, because the piece leaves your PROCESSOR through their PROCESSOR AREA (§5.5, §6.1). At the default rates an ALLIANCE whose own HUMAN PLAYER makes no throw scores 261.0 ± 2.5 instead of 274.8 ± 3.8, and crippling the opponent's HUMAN PLAYER swings the margin the other way by 25.9 ± 3.5 (281.1 ± 3.1 against 255.3 ± 2.4): the two ends of the chain are the same 4-point leg, seen from either side of it. Cutting their area limit to 1 ALGAE takes the MATCH's NET column from 16 to 13 and its G435 overflow count from 2 to 5 and reads 271.2 ± 3.0; slowing the throws to one per 4 s reads 273.5 ± 3.4 with NET 15 and overflow 3, because at 2.2 s the queue is not what is holding the NET back.

The CHUTE has one hard side and one soft one: slowing the feed to 3 s per CORAL reads 265.0 ± 3.6 while speeding it to 0.5 s reads 275.2 ± 3.9, against the default 1.4 s at 274.8 ± 3.8. Ten points of that is separable at n=24 and 0.4 is not, so what the model says is that a fleet of these tempos starves when the HUMAN PLAYER is slow and is already fed when it is fast — which is the opposite of the reading this card carried while the STATION queue was the binding constraint.

The NET's other feeder

§6.1 gives the NET two subjects: the HUMAN PLAYER throws into it, and a ROBOT scores in it. The build had only the first, argued from §5.4.2's 6 ft 4 in mesh against R104's 3 ft 6 in — but R104 measures the STARTING CONFIGURATION, not an elevator in play, and Table 6-2 pays the NET in the AUTO column, where G404 bars only the HUMAN PLAYER. So score-net exists, taken from beside the mesh because G420 forbids contacting it and §6.5.1 counts a ring only while no ROBOT of that ALLIANCE is touching it. The reach is a ROBOT property: NET lift s on the card, 0 = cannot clear the border. The shipped trio carries one — 1.9 / 1.4 / 2.05 s, each machine's own L4 placement time plus [estimate] 0.5 s, because a ROBOT that lands a CORAL at 72 in clears a border 76 in up and a 16 in ring is the wider piece. No 2025 source prints which machines had the height, so it is derived from the ledger that has been read line by line rather than asserted: 2025cmptx_f1m1's Johnson A1 totals 295 as 63 AUTO CORAL + 145 tele CORAL + 36 NET + 9 LEAVE + 36 DEEP + 6 FOUL, which is nine ALGAE in the NET and none in the PROCESSOR — those nets were lifted in, not thrown up. The golden fingerprint moved from fa67df3a's 293-305 to 65be7627's 251-247, and on that seed both ALLIANCES now bank 9 NET with every ring lifted in by a ROBOT and no PROCESSOR column at all. 0 stays the ablation.

The two arms disagree on purpose (n=24, mirror and head-to-head, seedBase 9000). The shipped trio against the same trio with the reach forced back to 0 reads 274.8 ± 3.8 / 273.1 ± 2.9 against 301.6 ± 2.7 / 301.0 ± 2.9, i.e. −26.8 ± 4.2 points a MATCH (23/24 scores differ). Forcing all six machines to one lift time instead: 1.2 s −45.0 ± 2.7 (24/24), 1.9 s −21.3 ± 4.0 (24/24, and −28.1 ± 3.5 on a second seed base, so the sign is not sample-dependent), 3 s −4.5 ± 2.2 (22/24). One side having it against a side without: blue 290.5 ± 2.1 against red 280.2 ± 3.2, margin +10.3 ± 4.0 and a paired shift of +9.7 ± 3.9 (23/24). Both being able to do it is worse for both, and one being able to do it is worth about ten points, because the ALGAE in your NET are the pieces your opponent pushed through their PROCESSOR (§5.5): a fleet that lifts its own rings in refuses to feed the goal it is scoring at — on the seed the table prints, the 1.2 s field runs PROC 8 / NET 18 with 10 of them lifted, against the control's PROC 18 / NET 17 with none — and withholds the 2-in-each-PROCESSOR Coopertition with them. Greedy bids the two routes on margin per second, and npm run verify's guard 21 holds the books shut over 16 MATCHes: the action fired in all 16 at 11.1 deposits a MATCH, every one of the 18 staged ALGAE still accounted for, no deposit ever made from the opponent's half, and 0 deposits on the same seeds with the reach forced to 0.

The physics

Same harness, but every arm is paired on identical seeds, because the seed spread of this engine is several points and two independent means would swallow a real effect. A ROBOT is R104's 120 in perimeter as a 30 in square, oriented by a heading integrated under its drivetrain; the REEF is its convex hexagon; LEAVE and PARK are judged on the BUMPERS (§6.5.2). Switching the spin ramp off costs +9.4 ± 3.0 points on the 9000 seed base with all 24 scores different and +6.8 ± 2.8 on the 21000 base (22/24), and the guard's 48-seed paired run reads 5.8 ± 2.2 with 45/48 different: turning costs time, and one seed base alone has called this noise before.

The bumper momentum exchange is worth +6.7 ± 2.9 points (+5.8 ± 3.5 on the second base, 0.6 ± 2.3 in the guard's 16-MATCH paired run) — which is not what this card used to claim, and the reason it changed is worth more than the number. It used to read +53 ± 14, because the thing the bounce was doing was shoving three machines out of the pile-up they fell into at their own CORAL STATION. That job is now done by an explicit escape (see the card below), so what is left of the impulse is what it physically is: pairs spend 3.2% of their frames in contact as shipped, and taking the exchange out moves that share by 0.67 ± 0.28 percentage points.

The HUMAN PLAYER's throws are the biggest single mechanism in this column: turning them off costs 18.4 ± 3.2 a MATCH over 24 paired seeds, 22/24 scores different. The drivetrain does buy throughput too. Every ROBOT on tank instead of swerve costs about 29 points a MATCH (253.6 against 282.8, each played as its own 24-MATCH mirror on both seats, and 253.8 against 275.5 in the guard's 16-MATCH run), because a machine that cannot strafe spends half a trip backing up and turning. Mecanum, which strafes at 0.62 of its forward rate, is level with swerve (280.2 against 282.8): at these tempos partial strafing is already enough, so the cliff the model finds is at "no sideways at all", not at "some". All of these fractions are estimates — R103 and R104 say nothing about drivetrains.

Why the two ALLIANCES score differently

A mirror MATCH used to be one process played twice: the same trio, a point-symmetric field, a deterministic tick. With Machine spread at 0 and a CORAL that cannot miss it still is, and over 96 seeds that arm tied outright 9 times — not a finding about the game, just the model having no way to tell the two sides apart. Two things take that apart: every ROBOT draws one tempo factor per MATCH (±20% by default, the Machine spread field), and each HUMAN PLAYER draws its own form. The spread alone takes the ties on that risk-off arm from 9 to 5. On the shipped arm, where a miss is its own draw per alliance, the same 96 seeds read a 9.4 ± 0.8 mean |margin| at ±0%, 11.0 ± 1.0 at ±10%, 13.5 ± 1.0 at ±20%, and 19.7 ± 1.4 at ±40%; the exact-tie count bounces 7 → 6 → 3 → 4 across those arms, which is noise at n=96, so the guard asserts the |margin| and not the ties. The results table prints the factor next to each ROBOT, so you can see which alliance got the slower machines.

The three real 2025 Einstein Field finals were 295-281, 274-285 and 276-293 (margins 14, 11, 17), so the shipped ±20% now sits inside the bottom of that band and ±40% above its top — a bigger claim about two copies of one design than is worth making. What keeps the two sides from separating further is that both still drain the REEF's branches — 12 of 12 at every level, level after level — and the difference between them is mostly the TROUGH, which is the column the placement model leaves short (11.2 of its 24 against 15.0 when nothing can fall off).

The other half of the symptom was the page itself: Play one match replays whatever seed is in the field, so clicking it twice is the same MATCH down to the last tick. New match rolls a fresh seed and plays it, and the status chip names the seed you are watching so a result you want to keep can be replayed or exported.

The pile-up that was faking a blowout

A CORAL STATION sits in the pocket where the ALLIANCE WALL meets the guardrail, three ROBOTS can all want the same one, and nothing in the engine or the baseline ever said "give up". So a machine would arrive, be pushed back by its own teammates, drive in again, and hold that spot — one of them still carrying a CORAL it had aimed at a branch for eighty seconds. Measured on the build where that was found, the 48-MATCH batch finished its weakest ALLIANCE on 13 of the 60 CORAL the field holds and its widest mirror gap was 132 points, between two identical trios. No 2025 field ever produced that; the Einstein finals were 17 points apart at their widest.

Now a ROBOT that is told to go, is more than 22 in from its waypoint, and closes less than 4 in over three seconds backs out along its own heading for 0.6 s and re-decides — which is what a driver does. Same batch on the corrected field: the weakest ALLIANCE floors at 34 CORAL, the 48-MATCH batch averages 543.5 and the widest mirror gap is 36 points; the backing-out manoeuvre was needed 11.2 times a MATCH. unstickOff on that same field floors the weakest ALLIANCE at the same 34 but reads 537.1 and 54, so the escape is worth 6.4 points of batch total and 18 points of the worst-case gap rather than the collapse the first version printed. unstickOff is the ablation, and the guard reads both ends: switched off the fall has to come back, the gap has to widen, and if it ever fired more than 40 times a MATCH it would be the engine driving rather than repairing.

The same reasoning is in the baseline for the REEF: a ROBOT that has held a CORAL for four seconds against a branch pushes on to the next level down rather than parking in front of it, because the highest free branch is not always the reachable one.

Does a CORAL stay on

§6.5.1 pays an L2-L4 CORAL only "if the BRANCH is inside the volume of the CORAL", and a 4 in BRANCH inside §5.7.1's 11 in piece leaves about 3.5 in of slop. So a placement is now two things: a per-level action time (placeSec — 0.4 s on the TROUGH, 0.65 at L2, 1.0 at L3, 1.4 at L4 on the generic trio, and editable on each ROBOT card) and a per-level chance of not staying on (placeDrop, L1 0.02 / L2 0.06 / L3 0.13 / L4 0.22, rising with how far off the branch the machine was facing when it started pushing). The push does not begin until the ROBOT is actually lined up — 26° of aim on the swerve trio, 20° on the tank machine, because at 45° off a car a foot out points 11 in wide of the pipe — and the unaligned seconds are spent turning, which is where half of the cost is. CORAL can miss is the switch; with it off nothing falls and no time is paid for a swing.

What that is worth (n=24 mirror MATCHes, seedBase 9000): the TROUGH goes from 15.0 of its 24 filled to 11.2, L2/L3/L4 do not move at 12.0 each, and 8.0 CORAL an ALLIANCE-MATCH ends up back on the carpet against 0.0. Paired on identical seeds the model costs an alliance −14.0 ± 3.1 points a MATCH, 23/24 scores different, and −14.6 ± 2.6 on a second seed base, 24/24. The column that goes short is the one the manual scores by contact rather than by slot, which is the reading TBA's playoff ledger gives too: its 2025cmptx alliance-sides averaged 15.1 trough CORAL, this fleet 11.2. One knob inside the model is worth naming: a ROBOT that has been held off a branch for 4 s aims one level lower, and turning that retry-giving-up off moves the same arms to 10.7 trough and 12.0 L4, with 8.6 CORAL fallen instead of 8.0 — a wash, roughly, and it stays because a machine parked in front of a blocked branch with a full gripper is not scoring either way. npm run verify holds the geometry: on the golden seed 89 placements were booked, every one begun within 23° of its branch, and 20 of them did not stay on; placementRisk: false hands back the replay this round's ablation was re-based to (7856316, 267-279, with 0 dropped CORAL in it).

What defense is worth

Three new actions, each priced rather than argued (npm run measure, 48 paired MATCHes over two seed bases). DEFEND names a seat that stops collecting and shadows the nearest opponent who is out of their own REEF or BARGE ZONE — pressing a car inside its own zone donates a MAJOR to the other alliance under G427, whoever started it. One defender takes −7.0 ± 1.7 points off the pressed ALLIANCE's total and costs its own ALLIANCE −5.9 ± 1.9; with §7.4.4 switched off the take is −5.5 ± 1.4 and the cost −6.4 ± 1.7, so the cards and the impairment are worth about 1.5 points of the take. Two defenders take −7.0 ± 1.7 off for −15.6 ± 2.9 of their own: the second seat buys nothing, it only doubles the machine that is not cycling. In this model defense is a negative-margin action, and the checkbox says so.

Stealing from the opponent's CORAL STATION and stripping their REEF are worth a fraction each, and they point in opposite directions: switching the theft option off costs the fleet 0.6 ± 0.4 of its own total and 0.2 ± 0.4 off the opponent, while switching the enemy REEF off gains it 0.8 ± 0.4. That is a result rather than a dead switch, and the NET reach made the second one smaller: a machine that can lift its own ring is not eager to cross the field for one it has to push through the opponent's PROCESSOR. Whether the machinery works at all is guard 24, which sends one machine there on purpose — RED's queue went from 18 CORAL left to 5 while BLUE's went from 15 to 57, 23 CORAL still got placed, and all 126 the MATCH stages are still accounted for — and takes the opponent's REEF from 9 rings to 0 while booking 18 in its own PROCESSOR.

The contact fouls are rare, and the number that says so is npm run contact: two mirror greedy fleets produced 27 collisions over 24 MATCHes on one seed base and 2 on the other (1.1 and 0.1 a MATCH), because each alliance works its own REEF. The fleet that has collisions is the one fielding a defender — 799 and 784 over the same two 24-MATCH runs, median 33 and 34, p90 112 and 109, p99 181 and 186 — so §7.4.4's damage floor sits at that p90 (100) and the tipping floor at its p99 (180; both were set against the wider press of an earlier build, p90 104/105 and p99 174/194, and the press that holds its stand-off now lands just under it). With one defender the trio assesses 0.23 fouls a MATCH (11 G423 over 48); the whole press bill is 4.5 and 4.9 fouls a MATCH for 26.5 and 29.3 points, and nearly all of it is 2.9 G427 (contact inside a protected zone) and 1.4 G418 (the opponent's CAGE), so a defence study in this game is mostly a geometry study. Guard 23 forces the rest of the mechanism on a press it drives itself: 6 calls in one MATCH, one per collision because §7.4.4's three clauses are mutually exclusive, a card on every one, and 3 ROBOTS disqualified for the remainder. G421 — two ROBOTS with their BUMPERS beyond the BARGE ZONE — never fires on shipped traffic (over 18000 frames, 883 held a single ROBOT across and none held a second), so guard 23b parks two machines past the opponent's BARGE ZONE on purpose and reads the MINOR then the MAJOR every 3 s.

Current state

This is Stage 1 plus the HUMAN PLAYER, the 2026 build's physics, a per-MATCH machine spread, the 2025 Einstein finalists as presets the two ALLIANCES pick separately, the Stage 2 planner selectable per ALLIANCE, the placement model (a CORAL has to be lined up and can still fall off), the deny actions (DEFEND, the opponent's STATION and REEF as sources, §7.4.4 and G421), and a shipped NET reach on all three machines. On seed 12345 the generic mirror comes out 176 CORAL + 36 ALGAE + 9 LEAVE + 30 BARGE = 251 against 172 + 36 + 9 + 30 = 247: 12 of 12 BRANCHES at every level on both sides, 10 and 7 of the trough's 24, all nine ALGAE on each side lifted into its own NET by a ROBOT with none processed, two DEEP CAGES and one SHALLOW. Against the one MATCH whose whole ledger has been read line by line — 2025cmptx_f1m1 — that trio over the guard's 48 alliance-sides (npm run verify) is at 42.7 AUTO CORAL points where Johnson took 63 and Newton 60, 8.8 LEAVE where both took 9, 11.9/12.0/12.0 of the three BRANCH rows where both filled all three, 9.2 trough CORAL where the finals left 23 and 21, 8.9 NET where both left 9, and 2.00 DEEP CAGES where both hung three. The PROCESSOR column is 3.4 rings (21 points) where that record carries none, down from the 8.9 rings / 54 points it ran before anything could reach the mesh, and the alliance mean with it is 269.9 against a playoff field that averaged 260.56 and topped out at 301.

What Stage 2 found before it built anything: npm run headroom ran the allocation knobs through the tick engine, and cycle rate looked worth nothing — every one of the REEF's 36 branch slots gets filled (12.0 on each of L4, L3 and L2 on both seed bases) while only 11.5 and 8.8 of the 24-piece trough do, so no plan can outwork the fleet on pieces. Two levers moved points. Committing to the CAGES with 6 s left instead of 20 s is worth +10.8 ± 0.9 and +11.9 ± 0.9 (n=24, two seed bases), and 12 s is worth +7.6 ± 0.3 / +8.0 ± 0.4.
